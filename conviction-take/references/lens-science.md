# Science, technology and health lens

For research findings, technical claims, health, environment, and reporting on any of them.

## Where the tell hides

- **The gap between the claim and the measurement.** What the study actually measured versus what the headline says. Surrogate endpoints, self-report, model output presented as observation, in-vitro results presented as clinical.
- **The population.** Who was studied and who the claim is being applied to. Mice, undergraduates, one hospital, one country, one cohort.
- **The comparator.** Against placebo, against standard of care, against nothing. This determines what the effect size means.
- **Absolute versus relative.** A relative risk reduction with a tiny base rate is the most common inflation device in health reporting.
- **Funding and preregistration.** Who paid, whether the outcome was specified in advance, and whether the analysis matches the registration.
- **Publication versus press release.** The university communications office writes the headline, not the authors. Find the paper.
- **Benchmarks in technical claims.** Who chose the benchmark, whether it was in the training data, and what the baseline was.

## Bear case angles specific to this lens

- **Base rate and prior.** How surprising this actually is given everything already known.
- **Replication.** Whether this has been reproduced, and the replication record of the field. Some fields have poor ones and it is fair to say which.
- **Effect size versus significance.** Statistically real and practically meaningful are different claims.
- **Scaling and deployment.** Whether it works outside the lab, at cost, at volume, with real users, under maintenance.
- **The engineering gap.** Feasibility demonstrated is not manufacturability. Name the specific step that is unsolved.
- **Incentive to overstate.** Grant renewal, a funding round, a pending publication, a stock position.

## Handling

Be willing to say a claim does not hold up. Also be willing to say the science is solid and the interesting question is commercial, regulatory or political instead, which is frequently the case and where the finance lens earns its place.
