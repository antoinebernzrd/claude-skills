# Venture and business lens

For companies, funding rounds, product launches, markets, industries.

## Where the tell hides

- **Specs.** A capability far larger, faster or more general than the stated product needs. The 850-foot drydock at a drone-boat company.
- **Hires and acquisitions.** A regulatory affairs head, a defense-cleared team, a licence, a bank charter. People and permissions reveal the roadmap earlier than product does.
- **Geography.** A site or an office that the stated customer base does not justify.
- **Contract structure.** Who bears the risk, cost-plus versus fixed-price, exclusivity, take-or-pay. Reveals bargaining position.
- **Customer concentration and who is missing.** Absent logos matter as much as present ones.
- **What the round size implies.** Raise sizes are chosen against a plan. Reverse-engineer the plan.

## Bear case angles specific to this lens

- **Margin structure.** Whether the required margin has ever been achieved in that activity by anyone. Name who tried and failed.
- **Demand shape.** Is demand bimodal, procurement-gated, budget-cycle-bound, or genuinely elastic. Government demand and consumer demand fail in opposite ways.
- **Unit economics at scale versus at pilot.** Pilots hide cost of service, support load and churn.
- **Capital intensity versus cost of capital.** Venture money funding an infrastructure asset destroys returns even when the asset is good.
- **The moat may be decorative.** If the real competition is on a commodity dimension (throughput, price, distribution), the differentiator is marketing.
- **Talent and physical constraints.** Skilled labour, fab capacity, permits, grid connection. Hard constraints that capital cannot compress.
- **Category timing.** Whether this is early, on time, or the fourth attempt. Name the previous attempts and why they failed.

## Comparables discipline

Always name the comparable explicitly and say why it is the right one. Compare to the business the company would become if it succeeds, not the one it currently resembles. If a company is priced above a listed incumbent's market cap on a fraction of the revenue, say so plainly and state what has to be true to justify it.
