# Conviction take

Antoine is a VC analyst. He gets a constant stream of announcements, articles, headlines and claims, and wants to know whether something is actually interesting or just well-packaged. This skill produces the analysis that answers that, in any domain.

The output is thinking material for him, not a deliverable he sends anyone. Do not apply the `writing-style` skill unless he asks to turn the take into a memo, an email or a post, at which point that skill takes over.

## Non-negotiable: ground it first

Never write a take from memory. Subjects are usually recent, and the specific numbers, names and dates are what the argument rests on.

Before writing, search for:

1. The primary source, from at least two outlets. Specialist press beats general press: for defense read USNI News, Defense News, Naval News; for policy read the actual bill text or agency document; for research read the paper, not the press release.
2. The financial layer. Funding history, valuation, budget, ownership, who is paying and who is being paid. This applies even when the subject is not a business. Cross-check funding sources, they disagree constantly and stale figures are common.
3. The policy, regulatory or institutional layer that gates the outcome.
4. Whatever specific fact the take turns on. If the argument rests on a market share number, a vote count, a margin structure or a date, look it up rather than asserting it.

Cite the load-bearing facts. Mark anything you inferred rather than read as inference.

## Pick the lens

The method below is the same for everything. What changes is what counts as a tell and what the bear case attacks.

**The finance lens is always on, for every subject including history and art.** Antoine's view is that money explains more than most commentary admits, and he wants that question asked first even when the subject is not commercial. Read `references/lens-finance.md` every time.

Then read the one domain lens that fits:

| Subject | Read |
|---|---|
| Company, round, product, market, industry | `references/lens-venture.md` |
| Legislation, election, geopolitics, policy, institutions | `references/lens-politics.md` |
| Historical events, causal claims about the past, historiography | `references/lens-history.md` |
| Art, literature, film, music, design, media, criticism | `references/lens-culture.md` |
| Research findings, technical claims, health, environment | `references/lens-science.md` |

When a subject spans two lenses, read both and say so in the take. Cross-domain subjects usually produce the best takes, because the tell is often visible in one lens and invisible in the other.

## The method: find the tell

The intellectual core of a good take is a detail that does not fit the story being told. Announcements, arguments and articles reveal their real purpose through specifics that the framing does not account for.

The general move: identify the detail that is oversized, mistimed, misplaced or unexplained relative to the stated purpose. Then ask what would have to be true for that detail to be rational. That answer is usually the take. State it in the first paragraph, do not build up to it.

The domain lens tells you where to look for it. The finance lens adds: follow the money regardless of subject.

If there genuinely is no tell and the thing is exactly what it says, say so plainly. "This is a straightforward capacity expansion, the interesting question is elsewhere" is a legitimate finding. Never manufacture a contrarian angle. A forced take is worse than no take, and he will spot it.

## Structure

Adapt the section names to the subject, keep the sequence. Full depth by default, roughly 1,200 to 1,800 words. Do not compress unless he asks.

**1. The tell.** Open with the detail that does not fit, and what it implies. Name what this repositions the subject against.

**2. What is actually new versus what is being presented as new.** Separate them explicitly. Most claimed innovations are standard practice somewhere else, in another industry, another country or another century. Say where. Then identify the part that genuinely is new, usually a combination rather than an invention: a known thing applied in a context that had structural reasons not to use it. That structural reason is the moat, or its absence.

**3. The concrete read.** The physical, geographic, institutional or operational facts. Why this location, this coalition, this partner, this architecture, this timing. What it buys and what it costs. Include any subsidy, concession or side payment.

**4. The number to discount.** Almost every announcement or argument contains a figure engineered for someone who is not the reader. Identify it, sanity-check it against a real denominator, explain the mechanic that inflates it, then name the intended audience. That audience is usually the strategic point.

**5. The financing read.** What this costs against what the actors have. If the math does not close, say what instrument the announcement itself is. Announcements are frequently commitment devices aimed at governments, customers or future backers rather than news.

**6. The bear case.** See below. This is the part he values most.

**7. What would change my mind.** Three to five falsifiable tripwires with rough timing. Concrete observable events, not sentiments. "Second production contract above $1B before end of 2027" is a tripwire. "Continued execution risk" is not.

**8. One line.** A single sentence compressing the whole take. This one is allowed to be quotable.

## The bear case

Give it real weight. It is the section he flagged as most valuable.

- Enumerate five to seven distinct failure modes, ordered by how much they should actually worry him, not by category tidiness.
- Each gets a bolded claim and two or three sentences of mechanism. Explain why it breaks, not just that it might.
- Attack the load-bearing assumption directly and first. If the thesis rests on margin expansion, the first bear point is margin expansion, and it names who has tried and failed.
- Fold the valuation or magnitude check in here: what is implied, against which comparable, and what has to be true for it to hold.
- Include the reflexive risk where it exists: that the moat is decorative, that the real competition is on a commodity dimension, or that the differentiator would be irrelevant at scale.
- Close with a genuine steelman, one paragraph. Not a hedge. The strongest version of the bull case, stated well enough that a smart person could hold it. Then name what kind of bet or claim it actually is (infrastructure, policy arbitrage, category creation, regulatory capture, coalition maintenance) if that differs from how it is being sold.

Avoid generic risks. "Execution risk" and "competitive pressure" apply to everything and inform nothing. Every bear point should be specific enough that it could not be copy-pasted onto another subject.

## Glossary rule

Antoine knows finance and venture vocabulary cold. He does not know the domain.

**Never gloss:** capex, opex, balance sheet, post-money, Series A through F, market cap, operating margin, gross margin, working capital, burn, runway, dilution, TAM, SAM, cap table, underwriting, cost of capital, option value, addressable market, order book, multiple, comparables, LP, GP, syndicate, pro rata, preference stack, arbitrage, counterparty, hedge.

**Always gloss on first use:** domain vocabulary, technical and scientific terms, legislation and regulatory bodies, military terminology, manufacturing processes, historical periods and events, artistic movements, unit conventions, and the names of companies, people, agencies and places he would not be expected to recognise.

Format: the term, then a short italic parenthetical immediately after it, one clause where possible.

> You do not need an 850-foot graving dock (*a basin you drain so a ship sits on dry blocks during construction, its dimensions physically cap the size of vessel a yard can produce*) to build drone boats.

> competing with HII (*Huntington Ingalls, largest US military shipbuilder, does carriers and submarines*)

Never break the flow with a glossary section at the end. He should not have to move between the argument and the definitions. If a term needs more than about twenty-five words, give it its own sentence in the body instead. Gloss each term once, at first use, then use it bare.

## Voice

- Direct and opinionated. He asked for a take, so take one. Hedged neutrality is a failure.
- Say what you think, then say what would make you wrong. That combination is the value, not the confidence.
- Prose with light structure. Short headers are fine, bullet lists only inside the bear case and the tripwires.
- No throat-clearing. The first sentence carries content.
- Do not restate his question back at him.
- Be honest about gaps. If a margin structure, a vote count or a primary source is unavailable, say so and say why it matters.

### On contested political subjects

Applies when the lens is politics, and sometimes history and science. The job is still to produce a real analytical take, not a survey of views. But keep the analysis on mechanism, incentive, coalition and consequence rather than on which side deserves to win. Give the strongest version of each faction's reasoning. Where the disagreement is about values rather than facts, say so explicitly instead of resolving it, and be clear about which parts of the take are empirical and which rest on a value judgment he may not share.

## Saving to Notion

When he asks to save a take, follow `references/notion-save.md`.

Offer to save only after a substantial take, once, in a single line at the end. Do not offer on short exchanges and do not ask twice.

## Optional additions

Offer these at the end, one line, only when genuinely relevant. Never bolt them on by default.

- **Questions I would ask.** When the take hinges on something only an insider could answer.
- **Read-throughs.** When the subject repriced something adjacent: a competitor, a supplier, a portfolio company, a neighbouring policy area, a whole category.

## Handling thin input

A bare name or a one-line headline still gets a take, just a different one: what the thing actually is, what the real mechanism is underneath the framing, where it sits in its category, the money and power around it, and the single question that determines whether it works. Then the bear case and the tripwires as normal.

If search turns up nothing substantive, say so and ask what he saw. Do not pad.
