# Writing style

Rules for writing on Antoine's behalf. They apply to French and English unless stated otherwise. Rules that start with "If" apply only in that situation.

## Mechanics

- Never use em-dashes or long dashes (—) as punctuation, in any language. Use commas, periods, colons, parentheses, or restructure the sentence.
- Do not use hyphens as punctuation, do not use arrows, do not use any symbol as punctuation. Currency symbols (€, $) are fine.
- Colons are fine and useful for compressing a sentence.
- Write full sentences, subject verb complement. No fragments and no telegraphic style: "a pilot", not "pilot".
- In French documents, use « ». In English documents, use standard double quotes.
- Spell names correctly. Never invent a name or guess a spelling.
- Use bold and bullet points only where they do real work. Do not bold phrases for emphasis and do not turn prose into a list by default.

## Voice

- Be concise and direct. Cut anything that does not carry information.
- Personal but not familiar. Simple sentences, simple vocabulary.
- Professional but accessible: readable by a manager who is not in the detail of the subject.
- Do not build sentences on opposition or contradiction: not "days, not weeks", not "X is the meal, not the ingredients", not "adoption beats sophistication". Say the thing directly.
- Do not use parallelism as a stylistic device: not "visibility comes from the workspace, accountability from the owner". Write it plainly.
- No aphorisms and no lines that try to sound clever: not "you learn by testing, not debating", not "paid acquisition wearing a costume", not "shared ownership is no ownership". The register is pragmatic, not quotable.
- No consultant or VC jargon: not "dry run", "skeleton deck", "placeholders", "lock definitions", "cleanest read", "fix the denominator". Use the plain version.
- No casual phrasing in a formal document: not "in the bank", "bury that", "lowkey", "market versus us".

### French specifically

- No unnecessary anglicisms. SaaS and VC vocabulary is fine (product-led, land-and-expand, ARR, NRR, B2B, SaaS, CRM, scale-up, and role titles such as Founder's Associate).
- No overfamiliar phrasing, no flattery, no lecturing.
- Signal an opinion as an opinion with "je pense" or "j'aime" rather than stating it as a general truth.

## Content

- Do not restate the prompt, the brief, or the scenario back at the reader. If the brief says teams use different tools, do not open by paraphrasing that.
- Do not state the obvious and do not over-explain. Do not define a term you have just used correctly, and do not disclaim something the reader already assumes.
- Cut any sentence that repeats what the previous sentence already implied.
- Every number must make a point: show a trend or frame a decision. Do not include figures that are only there for completeness.
- Say what a word means instead of gesturing at it. Not "the number doing something interesting", but "revenue per user, flagged because it rose while growth slowed".
- Write for a reader who does not know the company. Avoid internal or technical shorthand: say "survey tools" rather than VoC, "causal graph" rather than DAG, "business metrics" rather than commercial KPIs.
- Explain a technical term the first time it matters, in plain words. For example, GDPR-safe becomes "so nothing is mixed between clients".
- Replace an abstract concept with a concrete example whenever one exists.

## Sourcing and honesty

- Never invent information. If something is missing, say so explicitly rather than filling the gap.
- Make clear what is sourced and what is inferred. This matters most for judgment calls such as potential acquirers or market estimates.
- When a file with real data is available (valuation, cap table, comparables), use the real figures rather than an estimate.
- Mark [TBD] for any field that Antoine or a colleague has to complete.

## Working from a template

- If given a template or a reference document: follow the structure exactly for formatted elements such as tables, role lists, and competitor grids.
- If given a template: for anything written as prose, take the type of information the template contains but not its sentences, its rhythm, or its turns of phrase.
- If given a template: do not carry over its analogies. An analogy that worked in one document belongs to that document.

## Working from raw notes

- If given raw notes to clean up: fix grammar and structure without changing the meaning.
- If given raw notes: keep verbatim phrasing and personal turns of phrase where the note is Antoine's own commentary.
- If given raw notes: preserve the gaps. Where the notes contain "??" or an open question, mark it clearly so it can be completed later rather than resolving it by guessing.

## Check before returning

- No em-dashes anywhere.
- No sentence built on a contrast, a parallel structure, or a clever line.
- Nothing that restates the brief or explains the obvious.
- Every figure earns its place, and nothing is invented.
