# Checklist — research collaboration / joint development

Full deep-review checklist. This is the most IP-critical and most A&B-specific
contract type. Both sides contribute and **new IP is created jointly**, and there
is a built-in tension: academic partners want to **publish** and retain IP; A&B
needs to **own/protect** commercially critical IP and file patents first.

**This type is never a simple GREEN.** The two items that must never be missed are
**foreground IP ownership** (#3) and **publication vs. patent timing** (#7).

Apply every item; locate the concept, judge presence + adequacy, follow the
interactions, redline anything not ✅. Expect many **👤 needs-human** items —
here the skill's job is mainly to make sure nothing important is overlooked.

---

### 1. Parties & structure
- **Check:** who the partners are (university/CNRS/CEA/lab, company, consortium) and each party's role.
- **Good:** parties and roles clearly identified.
- **Flag if:** multi-party consortium with unclear responsibilities, or a party that may be a competitor. *Severity: medium; 👤 for strategic fit.*

### 2. Background IP
- **Check:** how each party's pre-existing IP is defined and protected.
- **Good:** background IP clearly identified/scheduled; each party keeps its own; a licence granted only as needed for the project.
- **Flag if:** background IP undefined (ambiguity about what's "already A&B's"), or A&B's background IP made available too broadly. *Severity: medium–high.*

### 3. Foreground IP (Results) ownership — CRITICAL
- **Check:** who owns IP created during the collaboration.
- **Good:** ownership is explicit; A&B owns — or has exclusive rights to — the results that are commercially critical to it.
- **Flag if:** ownership is silent (may default to the partner), or foreground IP defaults to the academic institution/partner. *Severity: critical.*
- **Redline:** assign commercially critical results to A&B, or secure exclusive rights (see #5).
- **Interacts with:** #4, #5, #9, #10.

### 4. Joint ownership mechanism (if applicable)
- **Check:** if results are jointly owned, is there a mechanism to exploit them?
- **Good:** joint ownership **plus** clear rules on who may license/commercialise, on what terms, and whether consent/accounting is needed.
- **Flag if:** "jointly owned" with **no exploitation mechanism** — under many laws this can paralyse commercialisation (joint owners may each need the other's consent). *Severity: high.*
- **Redline:** add exploitation terms, or move to sole ownership by A&B with a licence back for the partner's research use.

### 5. Licence to A&B (where A&B doesn't own results)
- **Check:** if the partner owns results A&B needs, what rights does A&B get?
- **Good:** at minimum an **exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free (or pre-agreed) licence**, or a **first-option to negotiate a licence** (common with universities), with field/sublicensing rights.
- **Flag if:** A&B gets only a non-exclusive licence, or no defined rights, to results it needs to commercialise. *Severity: high.*
- **Interacts with:** #11 (funding) — paying for work you can't use is a core failure.

### 6. Licence to background IP needed to use results
- **Check:** does A&B get rights to the partner's background IP embedded in the results?
- **Good:** a licence to background IP to the extent needed to use/exploit the foreground results.
- **Flag if:** A&B can't practise the results without separately licensing background IP it wasn't granted. *Severity: medium–high.*

### 7. Publication rights + patent timing — CRITICAL
- **Check:** the partner's right to publish, and A&B's protection against premature disclosure.
- **Good:** a **pre-publication review window** (e.g. 30–60 days) during which A&B can (a) remove its confidential information and (b) file patents before disclosure; delay for patent filing permitted.
- **Flag if:** partner may publish with **no review window / no patent carve-out** — a premature publication can destroy the patentability of core IP. *Severity: critical.*
- **Redline:** add a review window, confidentiality-redaction right, and a patent-filing delay.
- **Interacts with:** #8, #10 (grant open-access duties can force publication).

### 8. Confidentiality
- **Check:** protection of shared confidential information within the collaboration.
- **Good:** strong confidentiality that coexists with the publication mechanism (publish only after review/redaction).
- **Flag if:** weak confidentiality, or a conflict between "must keep confidential" and "free to publish". *Severity: medium–high.*

### 9. Personnel / inventors / students bound to assign
- **Check:** are the individuals doing the work (incl. PhD students, visiting researchers) obligated to assign their inventions to their institution so the IP terms actually hold?
- **Good:** the partner warrants its personnel are bound by equivalent IP-assignment obligations.
- **Flag if:** researchers/students are not bound — the ownership chain is broken and the IP clauses may be unenforceable against the actual inventors. *Severity: high.*

### 10. Grant-funding overlay (cross-check)
- **Check:** is the project (co-)funded by a public grant (France 2030, BPI, EU Horizon)? If so, the grant's IP/dissemination rules may override this agreement.
- **Good:** the collaboration terms are consistent with the grant's IP, access-rights, and dissemination obligations.
- **Flag if:** grant-funded but the agreement is silent on, or conflicts with, the grant rules. *Severity: high.*
- **Action:** also run `grant.md` and reconcile. **Interacts with:** #3, #7.

### 11. Contributions, responsibilities & funding
- **Check:** what each party contributes (people, equipment, money) and who funds what.
- **Good:** contributions defined; if A&B funds the work, A&B's IP rights reflect that (ownership or exclusive rights).
- **Flag if:** A&B funds materially but receives no ownership or exclusive licence/option. *Severity: high.*

### 12. Commercialisation / field of use / exclusivity
- **Check:** A&B's rights to commercialise results; any field-of-use limits; partner's reserved rights.
- **Good:** A&B can commercialise in its field; partner's reserved rights limited to internal research/teaching.
- **Flag if:** partner may license results to third parties (incl. competitors), or A&B's commercial field is narrowly limited. *Severity: high.*

### 13. Warranties & liability on results
- **Check:** warranties on results and liability allocation.
- **Good:** results provided "as is" (research is uncertain); liability limited both ways; A&B not over-exposed for research outcomes.
- **Flag if:** A&B warrants results or bears broad liability for research outcomes. *Severity: medium.*

### 14. Term, termination & survival
- **Check:** duration and what survives termination.
- **Good:** IP ownership, licences, publication controls, and confidentiality survive appropriately; results created before termination are handled.
- **Flag if:** licences/IP rights lapse on termination, or survival is unclear. *Severity: medium.*

### 15. Export control (foreign institution)
- **Check:** collaboration with a foreign institution involving controlled/quantum technical information.
- **Good:** export-compliance clause; consideration of whether the fundamental-research exclusion applies.
- **Flag if:** foreign partner + controlled technical exchange + no export clause. *Severity: escalate.*
- **Action:** route to a human for export-control assessment.

### 16. Data protection / research data
- **Check:** does the collaboration involve personal data or sensitive datasets?
- **Good:** GDPR handled; data-sharing terms defined; a DPA where one party processes for the other.
- **Flag if:** personal data shared with no GDPR treatment. *Severity: high.*

### 17. Publicity / use of name
- **Check:** can the partner use A&B's name/results in announcements?
- **Good:** only with A&B's prior consent; consistent with publication controls.
- **Flag if:** free use of A&B's name/results. *Severity: low–medium.*

### 18. Governing law & dispute resolution
- **Check:** governing law and forum.
- **Good:** French/EU law or acceptable neutral forum.
- **Flag if:** unfavourable jurisdiction. *Severity: low.*
